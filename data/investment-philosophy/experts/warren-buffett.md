# Warren Buffett

## Profile

- **Full Name**: Warren Edward Buffett
- **Known For**: Value investing, Berkshire Hathaway, "Oracle of Omaha"
- **Organization**: Berkshire Hathaway (Chairman & CEO)
- **Net Worth**: ~$130+ billion (2024)
- **Key Works**: Annual Shareholder Letters (1965-present)

---

## Investment Philosophy

### Core Principles

1. **Circle of Competence**: Only invest in businesses you understand
2. **Margin of Safety**: Buy at prices well below intrinsic value
3. **Economic Moat**: Seek companies with durable competitive advantages
4. **Management Quality**: Partner with honest, competent managers
5. **Long-term Orientation**: Hold wonderful businesses indefinitely

### The Buffett Way

> "Price is what you pay. Value is what you get."

Buffett combines Benjamin Graham's quantitative value approach with Philip Fisher's qualitative focus on business quality. He looks for:

- Simple, understandable businesses
- Consistent operating history
- Favorable long-term prospects
- Honest and competent management
- Available at attractive prices

## Filing Analysis Approach

### Daily Practice

Buffett spends 5-6 hours daily reading. When asked how to get smarter:

> "Read 500 pages like this every day. That's how knowledge builds up, like compound interest."

### Key Sections He Focuses On

1. **Business Description (Item 1)**: Understand what the company does
2. **Risk Factors (Item 1A)**: What could kill the business
3. **MD&A (Item 7)**: Management's story and candor
4. **Cash Flow Statement**: The "truth serum"
5. **Footnotes**: Where the bodies are buried

### What He Looks For

**In the numbers:**
- Consistent ROE > 15% over 10+ years
- Little or no debt
- High profit margins
- Low capital requirements
- Growing "owner earnings"

**In the narrative:**
- Management candor and honesty
- Clear explanations of both successes and failures
- Evidence of owner-oriented thinking
- Rational capital allocation

### Owner Earnings Concept

Buffett's preferred profitability measure:

```
Owner Earnings = Net Income
                 + Depreciation/Amortization
                 - Maintenance CapEx
                 - Required Working Capital Increases
```

This captures the true cash available to shareholders.

## Key Quotes on Filing Analysis

### On Understanding the Business

> "We start by looking at the reports of companies that we think we can understand... and then we see from that report whether the management is telling us about the things that we would want to know about if we owned 100% of the company."

### On Cash Flow

> "The cash flow statement is where the truth lives. WorldCom reported tremendous income growth, but if you looked at cash flow net of investing, they were actually hemorrhaging cash."

### On Management Quality

> "We want managers who, whether the news is good or bad, give us a complete report."

### On Competitive Advantage

> "In business, I look for economic castles protected by unbreachable moats."

### On Capital Allocation

> "The first rule is not to lose. The second rule is not to forget the first rule."

## What We Can Learn

### For Filing Analysis

1. **Read the business description first** - Don't dive into financials until you understand what the company does

2. **Look for candor** - Does management discuss failures as openly as successes?

3. **Follow the cash** - Compare net income to operating cash flow; large gaps are warning signs

4. **Think like an owner** - Would you want to own 100% of this business at the current price?

5. **Focus on durability** - Look for businesses that will be stronger in 10 years

### For Investment Process

1. **Stay within your circle** - Don't analyze businesses you can't understand

2. **Patience pays** - Great opportunities are rare; wait for the fat pitch

3. **Quality matters** - A wonderful company at a fair price beats a fair company at a wonderful price

4. **Ignore market noise** - Focus on business fundamentals, not stock price movements
