# Valuation Metrics

## Quick Answer

Valuation metrics compare a company's market price to its financial fundamentals. They help determine if a stock is cheap, fair, or expensive relative to earnings, assets, or cash flow.

---

## Price-to-Earnings (P/E)

### Definition

How much investors pay for each dollar of earnings.

### Formula

```
P/E = Share Price / Earnings Per Share

or

P/E = Market Cap / Net Income
```

### Types of P/E

| Type | Earnings Used |
|------|---------------|
| **Trailing P/E** | Last 12 months actual earnings |
| **Forward P/E** | Next 12 months estimated earnings |

### Example

| Item | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Share Price | 100 SEK |
| EPS (trailing) | 5 SEK |
| **Trailing P/E** | **20x** |

### Interpretation

| P/E | Signal |
|-----|--------|
| < 10 | Potentially cheap (or distressed) |
| 10-15 | Value range |
| 15-25 | Fair/growth |
| > 25 | Expensive (or high growth) |

### Context Matters

- High-growth companies deserve higher P/E
- Cyclical companies may have misleading P/E at peaks/troughs
- Compare to sector peers and historical average

### Red Flags

- [ ] Very low P/E with no obvious problems (hidden issues?)
- [ ] Very high P/E with slowing growth
- [ ] Negative P/E (company is losing money)

---

## PEG Ratio

### Definition

P/E adjusted for growth - is the P/E justified by earnings growth?

### Formula

```
PEG = P/E / Earnings Growth Rate

Example:
PEG = 20 / 15 = 1.33
```

### Interpretation

| PEG | Signal |
|-----|--------|
| < 1.0 | Potentially undervalued |
| 1.0 | Fair value |
| > 1.5 | Potentially overvalued |

### Caveats

- Growth rates are estimates
- Works best for consistent growers
- Doesn't account for quality of growth

---

## Price-to-Book (P/B)

### Definition

How much investors pay relative to book value (accounting equity).

### Formula

```
P/B = Share Price / Book Value Per Share

or

P/B = Market Cap / Shareholders' Equity
```

### Example

| Item | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Market Cap | 2,000M |
| Book Value | 1,000M |
| **P/B** | **2.0x** |

### Interpretation

| P/B | Signal |
|-----|--------|
| < 1.0 | Trading below book value |
| 1.0 - 2.0 | Typical range |
| > 3.0 | Premium valuation |

### When P/B Matters Most

- Financial companies (banks, insurance)
- Asset-heavy businesses
- Distressed companies
- Net-net value investing

### When P/B Is Less Useful

- Intangible-heavy businesses (tech, brands)
- High-ROE companies (should trade at premium)
- Companies with historical cost accounting

---

## Enterprise Value (EV)

### Definition

Total value of the business (debt + equity - cash).

### Formula

```
EV = Market Cap + Total Debt - Cash

or

EV = Market Cap + Net Debt + Minority Interest + Preferred Stock
```

### Example

| Item | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Market Cap | 2,000M |
| Total Debt | 500M |
| Cash | (200M) |
| **EV** | **2,300M** |

### Why Use EV?

- Represents what you'd pay to acquire the whole company
- Allows comparison of companies with different capital structures
- Basis for EV-based multiples

---

## EV/EBITDA

### Definition

Enterprise value relative to operating earnings (before interest, taxes, D&A).

### Formula

```
EV/EBITDA = Enterprise Value / EBITDA
```

### Example

| Item | Amount |
|------|--------|
| EV | 2,300M |
| EBITDA | 300M |
| **EV/EBITDA** | **7.7x** |

### Interpretation

| Multiple | Signal |
|----------|--------|
| < 6x | Potentially cheap |
| 6-10x | Fair range |
| 10-15x | Premium |
| > 15x | Very expensive or high growth |

### Why It's Popular

- Capital structure neutral
- Ignores non-cash charges (D&A)
- Good for comparing M&A targets
- Works across industries

### Caveats

- Ignores CapEx requirements
- Ignores working capital needs
- D&A matters for capital-intensive businesses

---

## EV/EBIT

### Definition

Like EV/EBITDA but includes depreciation.

### Formula

```
EV/EBIT = Enterprise Value / Operating Income
```

### When to Use

- Capital-intensive businesses where D&A is real cost
- More conservative than EV/EBITDA

---

## EV/Revenue (EV/Sales)

### Definition

Enterprise value relative to revenue.

### Formula

```
EV/Revenue = Enterprise Value / Revenue
```

### Example

| Item | Amount |
|------|--------|
| EV | 2,300M |
| Revenue | 1,200M |
| **EV/Revenue** | **1.9x** |

### Interpretation

| Multiple | Signal |
|----------|--------|
| < 1x | Very cheap (or troubled) |
| 1-3x | Typical |
| 3-5x | Premium |
| > 5x | Expensive or high-growth |

### When to Use

- Early-stage companies (no profits)
- Comparing within same industry
- SaaS companies (often 5-15x ARR)

---

## FCF Yield

### Definition

Free cash flow return on market price.

### Formula

```
FCF Yield = Free Cash Flow / Market Cap

or

FCF Yield = Free Cash Flow / Enterprise Value
```

### Example

| Item | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Free Cash Flow | 150M |
| Market Cap | 2,000M |
| **FCF Yield** | **7.5%** |

### Interpretation

| Yield | Signal |
|-------|--------|
| > 10% | Potentially very cheap |
| 5-10% | Attractive |
| 3-5% | Fair |
| < 3% | Expensive |

### Why FCF Yield Is Valuable

- Uses actual cash, not accounting earnings
- Can compare to bond yields
- Shows real return to shareholders

---

## Dividend Yield

### Definition

Annual dividends relative to share price.

### Formula

```
Dividend Yield = Annual Dividend Per Share / Share Price
```

### Example

| Item | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Annual Dividend | 3 SEK |
| Share Price | 100 SEK |
| **Dividend Yield** | **3%** |

### Interpretation

- Compare to risk-free rate
- High yield may signal distress (dividend cut risk)
- Growing dividend is better than high but flat

## Quick Reference

| Metric | Formula | Lower = Cheaper |
|--------|---------|-----------------|
| **P/E** | Price / EPS | Yes |
| **PEG** | P/E / Growth | Yes |
| **P/B** | Price / Book | Yes |
| **EV/EBITDA** | EV / EBITDA | Yes |
| **EV/Revenue** | EV / Revenue | Yes |
| **FCF Yield** | FCF / Price | Higher = Cheaper |

## Valuation Framework

### Step 1: Screen

Quick metrics to filter universe:
- P/E < industry average
- EV/EBITDA < 10x
- FCF Yield > 5%

### Step 2: Analyze

Deeper dive:
- Why is it cheap? (Justified or opportunity)
- What's priced in?
- What could go wrong?

### Step 3: Compare

- vs. Historical average
- vs. Industry peers
- vs. Expected growth

---

## Analysis Checklist

- [ ] Calculate key multiples (P/E, EV/EBITDA, FCF Yield)
- [ ] Compare to historical range
- [ ] Compare to industry peers
- [ ] Adjust for growth (PEG)
- [ ] Check if cheapness is justified
- [ ] Consider quality (cheap for a reason?)
