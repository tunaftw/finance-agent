# Profitability Metrics

## Quick Answer

Profitability metrics show how efficiently a company converts revenue into profit and capital into returns. They reveal pricing power, cost control, and competitive advantage.

---

## Profit Margins

### Gross Margin

**What it measures**: Profitability of the product/service itself (before operating costs).

```
Gross Margin = Gross Profit / Revenue
             = (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue
```

**Where to find it**: Income Statement

**Example**:
| Item | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Revenue | 1,000M |
| COGS | (600M) |
| Gross Profit | 400M |
| **Gross Margin** | **40%** |

**Interpretation**:
| Margin | Industry |
|--------|----------|
| > 70% | Software, luxury |
| 40-70% | Tech hardware, pharma |
| 20-40% | Manufacturing, retail |
| < 20% | Commodities, groceries |

**What it tells you**:
- High margin = pricing power, differentiation
- Low margin = commodity, competitive pressure
- Declining margin = cost pressure or competition

---

### Operating Margin (EBIT Margin)

**What it measures**: Profitability after all operating costs.

```
Operating Margin = Operating Income / Revenue
                 = EBIT / Revenue
```

**Example**:
| Item | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Revenue | 1,000M |
| Operating Income | 150M |
| **Operating Margin** | **15%** |

**Good values**:
- > 20%: Excellent
- 10-20%: Good
- 5-10%: Moderate
- < 5%: Thin

---

### Net Margin

**What it measures**: Bottom-line profitability after everything (including interest and taxes).

```
Net Margin = Net Income / Revenue
```

**Example**:
| Item | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Revenue | 1,000M |
| Net Income | 100M |
| **Net Margin** | **10%** |

**Context**:
- Generally 3-5% lower than operating margin
- Tax rates vary by jurisdiction
- Interest costs reduce net margin for leveraged companies

## Return Metrics

### Return on Equity (ROE)

**What it measures**: How much profit is generated from shareholders' investment.

```
ROE = Net Income / Average Shareholders' Equity
```

**Where to find it**:
- Net Income: Income Statement
- Equity: Balance Sheet (average of beginning and ending)

**Example**:
| Item | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Net Income | 100M |
| Avg Equity | 500M |
| **ROE** | **20%** |

**Interpretation**:
| ROE | Quality |
|-----|---------|
| > 20% | Excellent |
| 15-20% | Good |
| 10-15% | Average |
| < 10% | Below average |

**Buffett's view**: Look for > 15% ROE consistently over 10+ years.

**Caveat**: High debt artificially inflates ROE. Always check alongside D/E ratio.

---

### Return on Assets (ROA)

**What it measures**: How efficiently all assets generate profit (regardless of how funded).

```
ROA = Net Income / Average Total Assets
```

**Example**:
| Item | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Net Income | 100M |
| Avg Assets | 2,000M |
| **ROA** | **5%** |

**Interpretation**:
- Asset-light businesses (software): Can be > 15%
- Asset-heavy (manufacturing): Often 3-8%
- Banks: Typically 1-2%

**Why it matters**: Shows productivity of assets regardless of capital structure.

---

### Return on Invested Capital (ROIC)

**What it measures**: Return on capital invested in the business (debt + equity).

```
ROIC = NOPAT / Invested Capital

Where:
NOPAT = Operating Income × (1 - Tax Rate)
Invested Capital = Total Debt + Equity - Excess Cash
```

**Alternative formula**:
```
Invested Capital = Total Assets - Non-interest-bearing Liabilities - Excess Cash
```

**Example**:
| Item | Amount |
|------|--------|
| Operating Income | 150M |
| Tax Rate | 25% |
| NOPAT | 112.5M |
| Total Debt | 400M |
| Equity | 600M |
| Excess Cash | (100M) |
| Invested Capital | 900M |
| **ROIC** | **12.5%** |

**Why it's the best return metric**:
- Not distorted by leverage (unlike ROE)
- Shows true business returns
- Compare to cost of capital (WACC)

**Interpretation**:
| ROIC vs WACC | Meaning |
|--------------|---------|
| ROIC > WACC | Creating value |
| ROIC = WACC | Breaking even |
| ROIC < WACC | Destroying value |

## DuPont Analysis

### What It Is

Break down ROE into three components to understand what's driving returns.

### Formula

```
ROE = Net Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier

Where:
Net Margin = Net Income / Revenue (Profitability)
Asset Turnover = Revenue / Assets (Efficiency)
Equity Multiplier = Assets / Equity (Leverage)
```

### Example

| Component | Calculation | Value |
|-----------|-------------|-------|
| Net Margin | 100M / 1,000M | 10% |
| Asset Turnover | 1,000M / 2,000M | 0.5 |
| Equity Multiplier | 2,000M / 500M | 4.0 |
| **ROE** | 10% × 0.5 × 4.0 | **20%** |

### Interpretation

High ROE can come from:
1. **High margins** (good - pricing power)
2. **High asset turnover** (good - efficient operations)
3. **High leverage** (risky - debt magnifies returns)

## Trend Analysis

### What to Watch

| Trend | Signal |
|-------|--------|
| Margins expanding | Pricing power, cost control, scale |
| Margins stable | Mature, steady business |
| Margins contracting | Competition, cost pressure, trouble |

### Compare To

- Prior years (trend)
- Industry peers (relative position)
- Company guidance (execution)

## Quick Reference

| Metric | Formula | Excellent | Concern |
|--------|---------|-----------|---------|
| **Gross Margin** | GP / Revenue | > 50% | Declining |
| **Operating Margin** | EBIT / Revenue | > 20% | < 5% |
| **Net Margin** | NI / Revenue | > 15% | < 3% |
| **ROE** | NI / Equity | > 15% | < 10% |
| **ROA** | NI / Assets | > 10% | < 3% |
| **ROIC** | NOPAT / IC | > WACC | < WACC |
