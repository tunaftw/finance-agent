# Cash Flow Statement (Kassaflödesanalys)

## Quick Answer

The cash flow statement shows **how cash moved** during a period. It explains why cash on the balance sheet changed. Unlike the income statement (which uses accrual accounting), cash flow shows actual cash in and out.

> "Cash flow is the truth serum of financial statements." - Warren Buffett

---

## Why It Matters

1. **Earnings can be manipulated** - Cash flow is harder to fake
2. **Companies die from lack of cash**, not lack of profits
3. **Quality check** - Compare to net income to assess earnings quality

---

## The Three Sections

```
OPERATING ACTIVITIES (Löpande verksamhet)
Cash from running the business
→ Should be positive for healthy companies

INVESTING ACTIVITIES (Investeringsverksamhet)
Cash spent on/received from long-term investments
→ Usually negative (buying assets for growth)

FINANCING ACTIVITIES (Finansieringsverksamhet)
Cash from/to capital providers (debt + equity)
→ Direction varies based on strategy
```

---

## Section 1: Operating Cash Flow (OCF)

### What It Is

Cash generated from the company's core business operations.

### Two Methods of Presentation

**Indirect Method** (most common):
```
Net Income
+ Depreciation & Amortization (non-cash expense)
+ Stock-based Compensation (non-cash)
± Changes in Working Capital
  ± Accounts Receivable
  ± Inventory
  ± Accounts Payable
  ± Other current assets/liabilities
= Operating Cash Flow
```

**Direct Method** (rare):
```
Cash Received from Customers
- Cash Paid to Suppliers
- Cash Paid to Employees
- Cash Paid for Taxes
= Operating Cash Flow
```

### Key Adjustments

| Item | Adjustment | Why |
|------|------------|-----|
| Depreciation | ADD back | Expense but no cash out |
| Stock Comp | ADD back | Expense but no cash out |
| Receivables increase | SUBTRACT | Sold but didn't collect |
| Inventory increase | SUBTRACT | Bought but didn't sell |
| Payables increase | ADD | Bought but didn't pay |

### What to Look For

- **OCF should be positive** for established companies
- **OCF should track net income** over time
- If Net Income >> OCF consistently = **red flag** (accruals problem)

---

## Section 2: Investing Cash Flow

### What It Is

Cash spent on or received from long-term assets and investments.

### Common Items

| Item | Typical Sign | Notes |
|------|--------------|-------|
| CapEx (PP&E purchases) | (Negative) | Investment in growth |
| Acquisitions | (Negative) | Buying companies |
| Asset Sales | Positive | Selling PP&E or businesses |
| Investment Purchases | (Negative) | Buying securities |
| Investment Sales | Positive | Selling securities |

### What to Look For

- **Negative is normal** - Companies need to invest
- Sudden large positive = selling assets (one-time, watch out)
- CapEx trends relative to depreciation (maintenance vs. growth)

---

## Section 3: Financing Cash Flow

### What It Is

Cash from/to capital providers - lenders and shareholders.

### Common Items

| Item | Sign | Notes |
|------|------|-------|
| Debt Issuance | Positive | Borrowing money |
| Debt Repayment | (Negative) | Paying back loans |
| Equity Issuance | Positive | Selling shares |
| Share Buybacks | (Negative) | Repurchasing shares |
| Dividends Paid | (Negative) | Returning cash to shareholders |

### What to Look For

- Growing companies: May issue debt/equity
- Mature companies: Often negative (buybacks, dividends, debt paydown)
- Red flag: Issuing debt to fund dividends

---

## Key Metrics

### Free Cash Flow (FCF)

**The most important metric** - Cash available to shareholders after maintaining the business.

```
Free Cash Flow = Operating Cash Flow - Capital Expenditures

FCF = OCF - CapEx
```

**Example**:
- OCF = 500M
- CapEx = (150M)
- FCF = 350M

### Cash Conversion

```
Cash Conversion = Operating Cash Flow / Net Income
```

- **>100%** = Excellent (more cash than earnings)
- **80-100%** = Good
- **<80%** = Investigate (accruals issue?)

### Owner Earnings (Buffett's Measure)

```
Owner Earnings = Net Income
               + Depreciation
               - Maintenance CapEx
               - Working Capital Increases
```

This captures true cash available to owners.

## Complete Example

| Line Item | Amount (MSEK) |
|-----------|---------------|
| **OPERATING ACTIVITIES** | |
| Net Income | 858 |
| + Depreciation | 300 |
| + Stock Compensation | 50 |
| - Increase in Receivables | (120) |
| - Increase in Inventory | (80) |
| + Increase in Payables | 60 |
| **Operating Cash Flow** | **1,068** |
| | |
| **INVESTING ACTIVITIES** | |
| CapEx | (400) |
| Acquisitions | (200) |
| **Investing Cash Flow** | **(600)** |
| | |
| **FINANCING ACTIVITIES** | |
| Debt Repayment | (200) |
| Dividends | (150) |
| **Financing Cash Flow** | **(350)** |
| | |
| **Net Change in Cash** | **118** |
| Beginning Cash | 382 |
| **Ending Cash** | **500** |

**FCF Calculation**:
```
FCF = OCF - CapEx = 1,068 - 400 = 668 MSEK
```

**Cash Conversion**:
```
1,068 / 858 = 124% → Excellent
```

## Red Flags

- [ ] **OCF < Net Income** consistently - Accruals manipulation
- [ ] **Negative FCF with positive earnings** - Business consuming cash
- [ ] **Large working capital swings** - Smoothing or problems
- [ ] **CapEx << Depreciation** - Underinvesting (short-term boost)
- [ ] **Debt funded buybacks/dividends** - Unsustainable
- [ ] **Acquisitions masking organic decline** - Buying growth
- [ ] **Unusual "other" operating items** - What's hidden there?

---

## Quality Check: Net Income vs OCF

| Relationship | Signal |
|--------------|--------|
| OCF > NI | Good - Cash earnings |
| OCF ≈ NI | Normal - Balanced |
| OCF < NI (one year) | Investigate - Working capital timing? |
| OCF << NI (multiple years) | **Red flag** - Accruals problem |

## Swedish/IFRS Notes

### Terminology Mapping

| English | Swedish |
|---------|---------|
| Cash Flow Statement | Kassaflödesanalys |
| Operating Activities | Löpande verksamhet |
| Investing Activities | Investeringsverksamhet |
| Financing Activities | Finansieringsverksamhet |
| Capital Expenditures | Investeringar i materiella tillgångar |

### Key Differences

1. **Interest paid**: Under IFRS, can be in Operating OR Financing (check which the company uses)

2. **Dividends received**: Can be Operating or Investing under IFRS

3. **IFRS 16 Leases**: Lease payments now split:
   - Principal portion → Financing activities
   - Interest portion → Operating or Financing

## Where to Find It

### In 10-K (US)
- Item 8: Financial Statements
- "Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows"

### In Swedish Annual Report
- "Koncernens kassaflödesanalys"
- Usually after balance sheet
