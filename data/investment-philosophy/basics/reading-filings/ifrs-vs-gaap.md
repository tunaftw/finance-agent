# IFRS vs US GAAP

## Quick Answer

IFRS (International Financial Reporting Standards) and US GAAP (Generally Accepted Accounting Principles) are the two major global accounting frameworks. Swedish listed companies use IFRS; US companies use GAAP. While similar in many ways, key differences can affect comparability.

---

## Overview

| Attribute | IFRS | US GAAP |
|-----------|------|---------|
| **Issued By** | IASB (International Accounting Standards Board) | FASB (Financial Accounting Standards Board) |
| **Used By** | 140+ countries including EU, Sweden | USA, SEC registrants |
| **Approach** | Principles-based | Rules-based |
| **Reporting** | Typically PDF reports | SEC EDGAR filings |

---

## Key Differences

### 1. Revenue Recognition

**Current Standards**: IFRS 15 and ASC 606 are now largely converged.

| Aspect | IFRS 15 | US GAAP (ASC 606) |
|--------|---------|-------------------|
| Framework | 5-step model | 5-step model |
| Differences | Minor - timing in certain industries | Minor |

**Bottom line**: Revenue recognition is now largely comparable.

---

### 2. Inventory

| Aspect | IFRS | US GAAP |
|--------|------|---------|
| **LIFO** | Not permitted | Permitted |
| **Reversal of write-downs** | Allowed (up to cost) | Not allowed |
| **Costing** | FIFO, weighted average | FIFO, LIFO, weighted average |

**Impact**:
- US companies using LIFO may have lower inventory values and higher COGS during inflation
- Write-down reversals under IFRS can boost income

---

### 3. Research & Development

| Aspect | IFRS (IAS 38) | US GAAP |
|--------|---------------|---------|
| **Research costs** | Expensed | Expensed |
| **Development costs** | Capitalized if criteria met | Generally expensed |
| **Criteria for capitalizing** | Technical feasibility, intent to complete, ability to use/sell, etc. | Strict - mostly software only |

**Impact**:
- IFRS companies may have higher intangible assets
- IFRS companies may report higher operating income (less R&D expense)
- Watch for "capitalized development costs" in IFRS balance sheets

---

### 4. Leases (IFRS 16 vs ASC 842)

Both now require most leases on balance sheet, but:

| Aspect | IFRS 16 | US GAAP (ASC 842) |
|--------|---------|-------------------|
| **Classification** | Single model (all finance-type) | Two types: finance and operating |
| **P&L impact** | Front-loaded expense | Operating leases: straight-line |
| **Short-term exemption** | < 12 months | < 12 months |
| **Low-value exemption** | Yes (< $5,000) | No |

**Impact**:
- IFRS companies may show higher interest expense and lower operating expense
- IFRS depreciation + interest pattern differs from US operating lease expense

---

### 5. Impairment

| Aspect | IFRS (IAS 36) | US GAAP |
|--------|---------------|---------|
| **Indicator-based testing** | Required annually for all | Only goodwill annually tested |
| **Measurement** | Recoverable amount (higher of fair value or value in use) | Two-step for long-lived assets |
| **Reversal** | Allowed (except goodwill) | **Not allowed** |

**Impact**:
- IFRS companies can reverse impairments, boosting income
- Watch for impairment reversal income in IFRS statements

---

### 6. Financial Instruments

| Aspect | IFRS 9 | US GAAP (ASC 320/326) |
|--------|--------|----------------------|
| **Classification** | Amortized cost, FVOCI, FVTPL | HTM, AFS, Trading |
| **Expected credit losses** | Forward-looking from day 1 | CECL model (similar) |
| **Equity investments** | Generally FVTPL | AFS or FV with earnings election |

---

### 7. Contingent Liabilities

| Aspect | IFRS (IAS 37) | US GAAP (ASC 450) |
|--------|---------------|-------------------|
| **Recognition threshold** | "Probable" (>50%) | "Probable" (75-80% in practice) |
| **Measurement** | Best estimate (expected value) | Minimum of range |
| **Discounting** | Required if significant | Only if timing fixed |

**Impact**:
- IFRS may recognize provisions earlier
- IFRS provisions may be higher (expected value vs minimum)

---

### 8. Statement Presentation

| Aspect | IFRS | US GAAP |
|--------|------|---------|
| **Income statement** | By function or nature | By function |
| **Extraordinary items** | Prohibited | Prohibited |
| **Balance sheet order** | Varies (liquidity order or non-current first) | Liquidity order (current first) |
| **Cash flow - interest** | Operating, investing, or financing | Operating |
| **Cash flow - dividends received** | Operating or investing | Operating |

---

### 9. Goodwill

| Aspect | IFRS | US GAAP |
|--------|------|---------|
| **Amortization** | Not allowed | Not allowed |
| **Impairment testing** | Annual or when indicators | Annual or when triggers |
| **Testing level** | Cash generating unit | Reporting unit |
| **Impairment reversal** | Not allowed | Not allowed |

---

## Practical Comparison Tips

### When Comparing IFRS to GAAP Companies

| Adjustment | Why |
|------------|-----|
| Add back capitalized R&D to IFRS expenses | Comparable to US expense approach |
| Note LIFO reserve for US companies | Adjust inventory to FIFO equivalent |
| Check for impairment reversals in IFRS | One-time income boost |
| Normalize lease expense treatment | Different P&L patterns |

## Swedish IFRS Specifics

Swedish listed companies follow IFRS as adopted by the EU:

1. **K3 for private companies**: Simplified Swedish GAAP (not IFRS)
2. **Annual report format**: More prescriptive than IASB model
3. **Governance**: Additional Swedish corporate governance requirements
4. **Language**: Reports often in Swedish with English translation

## When Differences Matter Most

| Situation | Key Differences to Check |
|-----------|-------------------------|
| Comparing US vs Swedish company | R&D, impairments, lease treatment |
| Analyzing tech companies | Development cost capitalization |
| Analyzing manufacturers | Inventory (LIFO), impairments |
| Analyzing retailers | Lease accounting |
| M&A analysis | Provisions, contingencies |

## Convergence Status

IASB and FASB have worked on convergence for years:

| Area | Status |
|------|--------|
| Revenue (IFRS 15/ASC 606) | ✓ Converged |
| Leases | Mostly converged (minor differences) |
| Financial instruments | Similar concepts, different details |
| Insurance contracts | Both updated, some differences |
| Conceptual framework | Different but similar |
