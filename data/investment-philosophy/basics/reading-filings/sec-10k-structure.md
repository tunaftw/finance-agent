# SEC 10-K Structure

## Quick Answer

The 10-K is the annual report required by the SEC for US public companies. It's the most comprehensive source of information about a company. Understanding its structure helps you find what matters quickly.

---

## Filing Overview

| Attribute | Details |
|-----------|---------|
| **Full Name** | Annual Report Pursuant to Section 13 or 15(d) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 |
| **Filed By** | US public companies |
| **Frequency** | Annual (within 60-90 days of fiscal year end) |
| **Where to Find** | [SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&type=10-K) |

---

## The Four Parts

### Part I - Business Overview

| Item | Title | Content |
|------|-------|---------|
| **Item 1** | Business | Description of the company's business, products, markets |
| **Item 1A** | Risk Factors | What could go wrong (legally required disclosure) |
| **Item 1B** | Unresolved Staff Comments | SEC comments not yet resolved |
| **Item 2** | Properties | Facilities, real estate |
| **Item 3** | Legal Proceedings | Major lawsuits |
| **Item 4** | Mine Safety Disclosures | (Mining companies only) |

### Part II - Financial Information

| Item | Title | Content |
|------|-------|---------|
| **Item 5** | Market for Common Stock | Stock price, dividends, repurchases |
| **Item 6** | [Reserved] | (Previously selected financial data) |
| **Item 7** | MD&A | Management Discussion & Analysis |
| **Item 7A** | Quantitative Market Risk | Interest rate, FX, commodity risks |
| **Item 8** | Financial Statements | The full financials + notes |
| **Item 9** | Auditor Changes | Changes in accountants |
| **Item 9A** | Controls | Internal controls assessment |
| **Item 9B** | Other Information | Miscellaneous |

### Part III - Governance

| Item | Title | Content |
|------|-------|---------|
| **Item 10** | Directors & Officers | Board and executive info |
| **Item 11** | Executive Compensation | How much they're paid |
| **Item 12** | Security Ownership | Who owns the stock |
| **Item 13** | Related Party Transactions | Self-dealing disclosures |
| **Item 14** | Accountant Fees | Audit fees |

### Part IV - Exhibits

| Item | Title | Content |
|------|-------|---------|
| **Item 15** | Exhibits & Schedules | Supporting documents, contracts |
| **Item 16** | Form 10-K Summary | Optional summary |

## Most Important Sections (Investor Priority)

### 1. Item 1: Business (READ FIRST)

**What it contains**:
- Business description
- Products/services
- Markets and customers
- Competition
- Seasonality
- Regulatory environment

**Buffett's approach**: Start here. Understand the business before the numbers.

**Key questions**:
- Can I explain what they do simply?
- What drives revenue?
- Who are the customers?
- What's the competitive landscape?

---

### 2. Item 1A: Risk Factors (CRITICAL)

**What it contains**: Legally required disclosure of what could hurt the business.

**Why it matters**:
- Companies must disclose material risks
- Read year-over-year to see what's new
- Helps identify bear case

**What to look for**:
- New risks added this year
- Escalated language on existing risks
- Concentration risks (customer, supplier, geography)
- Regulatory risks
- Technology/disruption risks

---

### 3. Item 7: MD&A (MANAGEMENT'S STORY)

**What it contains**:
- Results of operations explanation
- Liquidity and capital resources
- Critical accounting estimates
- Contractual obligations

**Why it matters**:
- Management explains the numbers
- Shows how they think about the business
- Reveals what they're NOT saying

**What to look for**:
- How do they explain changes?
- Are they honest about problems?
- What are the key drivers?
- What's the outlook?

---

### 4. Item 8: Financial Statements (THE NUMBERS)

**What it contains**:
- Auditor's report
- Balance Sheet (2 years)
- Income Statement (3 years)
- Cash Flow Statement (3 years)
- Statement of Equity (3 years)
- Notes to Financial Statements

**The notes are critical**:
- Revenue recognition policies
- Segment information
- Debt details
- Lease obligations
- Pension assumptions
- Contingencies
- Related party transactions

---

### 5. Item 11: Executive Compensation

**What it contains**:
- Salary, bonus, stock awards
- Incentive plan details
- Employment agreements

**Why it matters**:
- Are incentives aligned with shareholders?
- What metrics drive bonuses?
- Any red flags (guaranteed bonuses, excessive perks)?

---

### 6. Item 13: Related Party Transactions

**What it contains**: Transactions with insiders, their families, or related entities.

**Why it matters**:
- Self-dealing red flag
- Important for fraud detection
- Jim Chanos: This is where Enron's fraud was hidden

## Reading Strategy

### Buffett's Order

1. **Item 1** - Business (understand first)
2. **Item 1A** - Risks (what could kill it)
3. **Item 7** - MD&A (management's story)
4. **Item 8** - Cash Flow Statement (truth serum)
5. **Item 8** - Footnotes (where bodies are buried)

### Quick Scan (30 minutes)

1. Search for "risk factors" changes
2. Read MD&A summary
3. Check cash flow vs net income
4. Scan footnotes for red flags

### Deep Dive (2-4 hours)

1. Full Item 1 and 1A
2. Complete MD&A
3. All financial statements
4. Key footnotes (revenue, debt, segments)
5. Compensation and related parties

## Red Flags to Search For

Use Ctrl+F to find:

| Search Term | Why |
|-------------|-----|
| "restatement" | Prior errors |
| "material weakness" | Control problems |
| "going concern" | Survival doubts |
| "related party" | Self-dealing |
| "off-balance sheet" | Hidden liabilities |
| "change in accounting" | Policy manipulation |
| "subsequent events" | Post year-end issues |
| "covenant" | Debt constraints |

## 10-K vs 10-Q vs 8-K

| Filing | Frequency | Content |
|--------|-----------|---------|
| **10-K** | Annual | Full year, audited, comprehensive |
| **10-Q** | Quarterly | Quarterly update, unaudited |
| **8-K** | Event-driven | Material events (acquisitions, departures, etc.) |

---

## Finding 10-Ks

### SEC EDGAR

1. Go to [sec.gov/edgar](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany)
2. Search by company name or ticker
3. Filter for "10-K" filing type
4. Click on filing date to access

### Company Website

Usually under "Investors" → "SEC Filings" or "Financial Reports"
